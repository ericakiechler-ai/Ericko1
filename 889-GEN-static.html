Before you inject

No phase shiftBoth CT sets sit on the same machine winding, so through current is a straight 180° opposition — nothing like the vector-group compensation a transformer differential needs. If the relay reports a differential on balanced through current, the cause is CT ratio, polarity or wiring, not compensation.
CT matchBoth ends are 5000:5, so a through condition gives equal and opposite secondary currents. Any difference the relay reports is real.
PolarityThe single most valuable outcome of this test is proving neutral-end CT polarity. A reversed neutral CT turns every through-fault into a full internal-fault differential and will trip the machine on the first external fault.

Method

How this tool arrives at its numbers

Everything below is stated so the arithmetic can be checked by hand. It does not replace the relay manual, the setting file or the machine data sheet — it replaces the conversion between them and the test set.

Reference frame

All applied quantities are secondary, at the relay terminals, and all angles are quoted against V L1 at 0° with counter-clockwise positive — the same convention as the CMC’s own phasor display. Triple A feeds the terminal-end CTs, triple B the neutral-end CTs.

Base quantities

I rated primary = MVA / (√3 · kV) = 4183.7 A Z base primary = kV² / MVA = 1.904 Ω Z base secondary = Z base primary · CTR / VTR = 16.56 Ω V L-N nominal = VT secondary / √3 = 69.28 V

That last conversion is the one everything on the impedance plane rests on. A reactance quoted in per unit on the machine base becomes X(pu) · Z base secondary at the relay, so this machine’s Xd of 1.80 pu is 29.81 Ω as the relay measures it.

Placing an impedance

A balanced three-phase set with voltage on 0° and current on −∠Z presents an apparent positive-sequence impedance of exactly |Z|∠Z. One magnitude is held and the other follows:

hold current: V = I · |Z| hold voltage: I = V / |Z| angles: ∠V = 0°, ∠I = −∠Z

Holding current is usually the better choice on a generator relay, because the impedances of interest run from a fraction of an ohm out to tens of ohms and holding voltage would drive the current past the amplifier at the small end. The Setup tab carries the choice and the held magnitude, and the configuration checks flag when either limit is about to be crossed.

For a phase-to-phase loop the same idea applies to Z = (V L2 − V L3) / (I L2 − I L3): V L2 and V L3 are put equal and opposite about 0°, the two currents likewise, and the healthy phase is parked at +90°.

Loss of excitation — 40

Offset mho below the R axis. With offset the distance from the origin down to the top of the circle and diameter its size:

centre = ( 0 , −(offset + diameter/2) ) radius = diameter / 2 boundary at θ = centre + radius∠θ

Zone 1 is conventionally offset by X′d/2 with a diameter of 1.0 pu; zone 2 keeps the offset and opens the diameter to Xd. The characteristic sits below the axis because that is where the machine goes when it loses field and starts drawing VARs — not because of anything about a fault.

Distance backup — 21

Mho through the origin with its diameter along the characteristic angle. Along a ray at φ the boundary sits at |Z| = reach · cos(φ − MTA), so a point placed at full reach 30° off the angle will not operate — the boundary there is 86.6% of the reach.

Negative sequence — 46

t = K / I2(pu)²

K is the machine’s published short-time unbalance capability, off the generator data sheet. A single-phase injection produces I2 = I/3, so the applied current is three times the intended I2; a balanced set in reverse rotation gives I2 = I with no positive- or zero-sequence content and is the cleaner test where the set can do it.

Stator ground — 59N and 27TN

A ground fault x% up from the neutral drives x% of the phase-neutral voltage across the grounding transformer primary, so at the relay:

V neutral = (kV / √3) · (x/100) / NGT ratio terminal fault → 239.9 V at the relay

That is why 59N goes blind near the neutral: the driving voltage falls to nothing. 27TN covers the remaining band by watching the machine’s own third-harmonic neutral voltage disappear, which is the one element on the relay where removing a quantity is what makes it trip.

Voltage-restrained overcurrent — 51V

effective pickup = pickup · clamp( V / V nominal , floor , 1.0 )

Timing multiples are taken against the effective pickup at each voltage, so “2×” means a different current at every point in the table.

Thermal and inverse-time

IEEE C37.112 t = TDM · [ A / (M^p − 1) + B ] IEC 60255 t = TDM · [ K / (M^E − 1) ] IAC / ANSI t = TDM · [ A + B/(M−C) + D/(M−C)² + E/(M−C)³ ] Thermal 49 t = τ · ln[ (I² − Ip²) / (I² − (k·Ib)²) ]

What this tool does not do

  • 64F field ground. The 889 detects it by injecting onto the field circuit through a dedicated coupling module. There is nothing for a CMC to inject, so it is not modelled here — that test belongs to the module’s own procedure.
  • 60 VT and CT supervision. The logic varies enough between firmware revisions that a generic model would mislead rather than help.
  • Setting file import. Settings are entered by hand, which means they get read off the relay once and checked.
  • Saturation, burden and lead resistance. Channel warnings are about the amplifier’s limits, not about whether the CT circuit will behave.

Before the first use on a job

  • Enter the real machine, CT and VT data and confirm the derived rated current and base impedance against the relay’s own metering with load on.
  • Run one 51 timing point against the relay’s predicted operate time. If they agree, the curve constants here match your firmware.
  • Run the 87G through-fault point and confirm near-zero differential. That single test proves the two CT ratios and, more importantly, the neutral-end CT polarity.
  • Measure the 40 boundary at two angles and check the two agree with one circle. If they do not, the offset and diameter conventions in the relay are not the ones assumed here.
