CT matchBoth windings land within 4% of each other at rated load — 3.35 A and 3.49 A secondary.
Amp modeTwo triples, 12.5 A per channel. Triple A feeds W1, triple B feeds W2 throughout this tool.
Tap range vs slope 1The tap changer moves W1 by ±10.0% about mid-tap. With 5% for CT error and 5% for the relay's own matching, slope 1 has to sit above about 20% or a healthy transformer at the end of its range will look like an internal fault. Before you inject

PolarityREF is a polarity test as much as a level test. If the stability point operates, the ground CT or one phase CT is reversed — chase the wiring before touching the settings.
Ratio mismatchPhase CTs are 1200:5 and the ground CT is 200:5. Both inputs are scaled to the same primary current: 20.000 A into the phase input is 4800 A primary, which is 120.000 A out of the ground CT. Both inputs have to fit the 12.5 A channel limit, and the ground side reaches it first: the largest through-current this CT pair can be tested at is about 0.42 ×CT. Above that the stability point cannot be produced by this amplifier at all — prove REF stability with primary injection, or accept a reduced through level and say so on the record.

Method

How this tool arrives at its numbers

Everything below is stated so the arithmetic can be checked by hand. Nothing here replaces the relay manual or the setting file in service — it replaces the twenty minutes of scratch-paper conversion between them and the test set.

Reference frame

All applied quantities are secondary, at the relay terminals, and all angles are quoted against V L1 at 0° with counter-clockwise positive. That matches the phasor convention in the CMC’s own display, so what you read here is what you type in.

V L-N nominal = VT secondary / √3 (wye) = 69.28 V CT secondary at rating = MVA / (√3 · kV · CTR)

Overcurrent pickup

A pickup in ×CT is referred to the CT rating, so it converts two ways at once:

I secondary = pickup × CT secondary rating I primary = pickup × CT primary rating

Where the operating quantity is not the phase current itself, the applied current differs from the setting. A single-phase injection produces a residual 3I0 equal to the applied current, but a negative-sequence I2 of only one third of it — so negative-sequence elements are tested at three times the setting, and the channel table already carries that factor.

Inverse-time curves

IEEE C37.112 t = TDM · [ A / (M^p − 1) + B ] IEC 60255 t = TDM · [ K / (M^E − 1) ] IAC / ANSI t = TDM · [ A + B/(M−C) + D/(M−C)² + E/(M−C)³ ]

M is the multiple of pickup. Curve constants have changed between firmware revisions on more than one product line, so treat the first predicted time as a check against the relay’s own curve, not as the reference.

Percent differential

Per-unit is referred to each winding’s own rated current, which is what makes the two secondary currents come out different:

I(pu) = I secondary · CTR / I rated primary I diff = | IW1(pu) + IW2(pu) · 1∠(−shift) | I rest = max( |IW1(pu)| , |IW2(pu)| ) (selectable)

The dual-slope characteristic is taken as flat at minimum pickup up to break 1, slope 1 from there to break 2, and slope 2 continuing from the value slope 1 reached at break 2. Each restraint point is then solved backwards to a pair of winding currents and offset by the margin, giving one point that must operate and one that must not.

The W2 triple is injected at 180° plus the vector-group shift. Through current then lands exactly on the relay’s internal compensation and nets to zero differential — which is why the balanced three-phase set is the right way to test a phase-shifting transformer, and a single-phase injection is not.

Directional elements

ECA is treated as the angle by which the operating current lags its polarising reference at maximum forward torque. Phase elements are polarised in quadrature, ground elements on −3V0. Both references are placed on 0° by the injections built here, so the forward maximum-torque angle comes out as −ECA and the boundaries sit ±90° either side. Sign conventions differ between manufacturers: prove forward and reverse once, and the four boundary points follow.

Volts per hertz

V required = (V/Hz)pu · V base · f test / f nominal

Which is the useful part of the element: the same per-unit V/Hz is reachable at reduced frequency and much lower voltage, keeping the amplifier well inside its range.

Thermal overload

t = τ · ln[ (I² − Ip²) / (I² − (k·Ib)²) ]

The IEC 60255-8 replica, with Ip the prior load. The replica has memory, so the second point in a run reads short unless the image is reset or given several time constants to decay.

What this tool does not do

  • It does not read or write relay setting files. Settings are entered by hand, which means they get read off the relay once and checked.
  • It does not cover 60 CT/VT supervision. The logic varies enough between firmware revisions that a generic model would be misleading rather than helpful.
  • It does not model CT saturation, burden, or lead resistance. Channel warnings are about the amplifier’s limits, not about whether the CT circuit will behave.
  • It does not know your relay’s firmware. Curve constants, the restraint definition and the ECA sign are the three places where a revision difference will show up first.

Before the first use on a job

  • Enter the real transformer, CT and VT data on the Setup tab and confirm the derived rated currents against the relay’s own metering with load on.
  • Run one timing point and compare against the relay’s predicted operate time. If they agree, the curve constants in this tool match your firmware.
  • Run the 87T through-fault point and confirm the relay reads near-zero differential. That single test proves the vector group, the CT ratios and the compensation all at once.
