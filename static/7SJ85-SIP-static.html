Configuration checks

RE/RL ≠ XE/XLThe two ratios come out at 0.9463 and 0.6957 — a 36% difference, because the zero-sequence R/X ratio is not the same as the positive-sequence one. That is normal on an overhead line and it is exactly why Siemens splits the ground return into two real numbers instead of one complex k0. Enter both in DIGSI; a device set with a single figure applied to each will measure ground loops wrong.
Ground-fault dutyThe network is set as resonant earthed, so 67Ns wattmetric carries the ground-fault duty with cos φ measurement. A solid ground fault puts 100.0 V of displacement voltage on the device. Fault current on such a network is a few amps primary, so 50N thresholds set for a solidly earthed system will never see it.
Z test rangeHolding 1.00 A needs up to 5.0 V for a ground fault at the remote end — inside the 300 V channel limit.
1 A secondaryWith 1 A CTs every current on this device is small: a 2.5 × In threshold is 2.5 A, and the sensitive ground thresholds run into the milliamps. Headroom is not the constraint here — resolution and amplifier accuracy at low output are.

Why this componentcos φ measurement suits a resonant-earthed network: the Petersen coil cancels the capacitive current, so what is left on the faulted feeder is a small resistive residual in phase with the displacement voltage. Against the networkThe Setup tab has this network as resonant earthed and the function is set to cos φ. That is the expected pairing.
MilliampsThresholds on this function run to tens of milliamps at the CT secondary. Confirm the amplifier's accuracy at that output, and confirm the core-balance CT is not shorted or left with its test link open — both look like a failed test and neither is one.
PolarityA reversed core-balance CT, or a CT star point set the wrong way on the Setup tab, turns every forward point into a reverse one. The forward and reverse pair are the two that prove it; the sector-edge points only refine the angle.

Method

How this tool arrives at its numbers

Everything below is stated so the arithmetic can be checked by hand. It does not replace the device manual, the DIGSI 5 parameter set or the network study — it replaces the conversion between them and the test set.

Reference frame

All applied quantities are secondary, at the device terminals, and all angles are quoted against V L1 at 0° with counter-clockwise positive — the same convention as the CMC’s own phasor display.

Z secondary = Z primary · CTR / VTR = × 2.0000 Vn secondary = VT secondary / √3 = 57.74 V In secondary = 1 A

Ground return: two ratios, not one factor

Siemens parameterises the ground loop as two separate real ratios where most devices take a single complex factor:

RE/RL = (R0 − R1) / (3 · R1) = 0.9463 XE/XL = (X0 − X1) / (3 · X1) = 0.6957 k0 = (Z0 − Z1) / (3 · Z1) = 0.7234 −5.8°

The two forms agree only when R0/R1 equals X0/X1. On a real overhead line they rarely do, which is the point of the split: it lets the device compensate resistance and reactance independently. Enter both in DIGSI. A device set with one figure applied to each will measure ground loops wrong in a way that grows with distance and never shows up on a phase-loop test.

Placing a fault on the line

phase loop Z = (V L2 − V L3) / (I L2 − I L3) no ground term ground loop Z = V L1 / (I L1 + k0 · 3I0) hold current: V = I · |Z loop| angles: ∠V = 0°, ∠I = −∠Z loop

A single-phase injection makes 3I0 equal to the phase current, so the ground-loop denominator becomes I · (1 + k0) and the applied voltage is the line impedance multiplied by |1 + k0| — here 1.7212.

Directional ground fault on a compensated network — 67Ns

This is the function that separates a European distribution device from a North American one, and the arithmetic is short:

φ = angle of 3I0 measured against −3V0 active = |3I0| · cos φ reactive = |3I0| · sin φ operate when the measured component exceeds its threshold, with 3V0 and 3I0 both above their own supervision levels

Which component is measured follows the neutral treatment, and getting the pairing wrong is the commonest setting error on these systems:

  • Resonant earthed — the Petersen coil cancels the capacitive current, leaving a small resistive residual on the faulted feeder in phase with the displacement voltage. cos φ finds it and ignores every healthy feeder’s capacitance.
  • Isolated — there is no coil, the residual is capacitive, and the faulted feeder carries the sum of every other feeder’s capacitance at 90° to the displacement voltage. sin φ is what separates it.

Thresholds run to tens of milliamps at the CT secondary, so the core-balance CT, its polarity and the amplifier’s accuracy at low output matter more here than anywhere else on the device.

Displacement voltage — 59N

The phase voltages for a partial ground fault are not three independent numbers. The neutral shifts toward the faulted phase by the fraction under test and the healthy phases follow:

V Lk = Vn∠θk − f · Vn∠0 3V0 = 3 · f · Vn at f = 1: L1 → 0, L2 and L3 → 100.0 V

Drive three unrelated magnitudes and the device will compute a 3V0 you did not intend.

Inverse-time characteristics

IEC 60255 t = T · [ K / (M^E − 1) ] ANSI/IEEE t = T · [ A / (M^p − 1) + B ]

Check the first predicted time against the device’s own figure in DIGSI before accepting the rest. Where disk emulation reset is selected, allow the full reset time between timing shots — the integrator runs down at a finite rate and consecutive points otherwise read short.

What this tool does not do

  • CFC logic. Configurable logic charts are tested by driving their inputs and watching their outputs; there is no generic model of a chart somebody else wrote.
  • Transient ground-fault detection. The transient method works on the first microseconds of the fault, which a steady-state test set cannot reproduce. 67Ns as modelled here is the steady-state directional function.
  • Trip-circuit supervision beyond a checklist. 74TC is a DC continuity test at the terminal block, and its rows carry no channels for that reason.
  • Setting-file import. Settings are entered by hand, which means they get read off the device once and checked.

Before the first use on a job

  • Enter the real line constants and confirm RE/RL, XE/XL and k0 against both the line-constants sheet and DIGSI.
  • Confirm the CT star-point direction in DIGSI matches the drawing before any directional test. It inverts every forward decision and no angle adjustment compensates for it.
  • Run one 51 timing point against the device’s own prediction to confirm the characteristic constants.
  • On a compensated or isolated network, prove 67Ns forward and reverse before spending time on the boundary angles — a reversed core-balance CT fails both and looks like a threshold problem.
