# 850-FDR — calculation review and QC record

**Tool:** `src/850-fdr.html` — GE Multilin 850 feeder protection × Omicron CMC 356
**Scope:** every `compute()`, the fault-placement model, the channel mapper, the limit
checks, the vector calculator, and the sample configuration. Each injected quantity was
traced from setting to channel with the sample ratios, and the notes were checked against
what the code actually does.
**Outcome:** released. Two medium findings and three low ones fixed; one item left open
for the manual.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | **B–C fault construction carried 3V0 = full L-N voltage.** `faultInject` placed Vb = Vm∠0, Vc = Vm∠180, Va = N∠90, so Va+Vb+Vc = N. The 21P loop (Vb−Vc)/(Ib−Ic) was correct, but the injection drove a 69 V zero-sequence voltage on a *phase* fault — above the 59N default pickup and into the 67N polarising path — during every 21P, locator B–C and vector-preset test. | Vb = −Va/2 + Vm·û, Vc = −Va/2 − Vm·û (û = prefault Vbc direction). Loop unchanged; V0 = 0; Vb = Vc = −Va/2 at a bolted fault, as the physics says. |
| 2 | Medium | **Phase rotation ignored** outside `P3()`: single-phase fault offsets, 46BC, 47, 59N, 67N polarising set, 27/59 single-phase points, the load preset and `seq()` all hardcoded ABC. On an ACB relay: 46BC's I2/I1 inverts; 47's reversal point is positive sequence and does not operate; the vector calculator swaps I1 and I2. | `rotS()`/`rotA()` helpers used everywhere; `seq()` exchanges p and n under ACB. |
| 3 | Low | Sheet reported B–G / C–G current angle as −∠Z without the phase offset. | Sheet reports the injected current's angle. |
| 4 | Low | Ground and core-balance elements injected on `I L1` with no note saying where it goes. | Channel note: *into the ground CT input* / *through the core-balance CT window*. |
| 5 | Trivial | Duplicated `inputmode` attribute; no Setup-first on a fresh load; k0 override hint did not say the angle is taken as 0°. | Fixed. |

## Open — needs the 850 manual

**IAC Extremely Inverse, constant E.** The tool carries E = −0.2461; GE's published
IAC table has E = +0.2461 for that curve (A 0.0040, B 0.6379, C 0.6200, D 1.7872). The
845 carries the same value. The difference is about 14% at 2× pickup and negligible above
4×. Not changed, because it could not be confirmed offline. Confirm against the 850
instruction manual's curve table and correct in `CURVES` in every tool that lists it.
The tool's existing guidance — check the first timing point against the relay's own
prediction — covers the customer in the meantime.

## Verified correct (no change)

Secondary-ohm factor CTR/VTR; k0 = (Z0−Z1)/3Z1 and the ground loop Va/(Ia + k0·3I0) with
its (1+k0) factor for single-phase injection; hold-current and hold-voltage modes; 46BC's
closed form f = (1−2r)/(1+r) (I1 = (2+f)/3 and I2 = (1−f)/3 re-derived); 47 by both
methods; 59N by both methods and the solid-fault case; 32 three-phase secondary watts and
the MTA current angle; IEEE C37.112 and IEC 60255 constants; 50BF, 37, 79, CLPU and 68
sequencing; 25 including dead-line/dead-bus; 81 and 81R; the channel-limit checks and the
Z-test range warning; the Setup-tab sanity notes.

## Accepted simplifications (documented, not changed)

- k0 override is a magnitude at 0°. Real k0 angles on overhead lines are a few degrees.
- 79 single-phase fault states hold the faulted-phase voltage at nominal; the sequence,
  not the magnitude, is what the element test proves.
- The core-balance CT secondary is assumed equal to the phase CT secondary.

## Verification

`tests/850.test.mjs` — 31 checks. Builds the tool, mints a licence, drives it from
`file://` with all network blocked, renders all 27 elements, and checks the 21G/21P loops,
FLOC, 46BC (via an independent sequence-component calculation on the injected set), 32,
59N, 50SG and the rotation behaviour against an independent implementation of the same
physics written in the test from the sample constants. Licence, evaluation and forged
states, decimal entry, and sheet provenance are covered as for the 845.
