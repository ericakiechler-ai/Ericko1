# 869-MTR — calculation review and QC record

**Tool:** `src/869-mtr.html` — GE Multilin 869 motor protection × Omicron CMC 356
**Scope:** every `compute()`, the thermal model, the channel mapper, the limit checks,
the vector calculator, the presets and the sample configuration. Each injected quantity
was traced from setting to channel with the sample ratios (200:5 phase, 50:5 core
balance, 4160:120 VT, 180 A FLA → 4.500 A secondary), and the notes were checked
against what the code actually does.
**Outcome:** released. Two medium findings and four low ones fixed; two items left
open for the manual.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Medium | **46 current unbalance ignored the relay's below-FLA definition.** The tool solved one reduced phase for I2/I1 = setting. GE's 469/869 unbalance quantity is I2/I1 only when the average current is at or above FLA; below FLA it is I2/FLA, precisely so a lightly loaded motor does not trip on a ratio that carries no heat. At the default 1.0 ×FLA load the reduced set has an average of 0.83 FLA, so the relay saw 13.8% at the "above threshold, OPERATE" point of a 15% setting — a false failure on every default-setting test. | Closed-form solve for both regimes (they split cleanly at load ≥ 1 + r because the reduced phase is in phase with the others, so I_avg = I1): f = (1−2r)/(1+r) above, f = 1 − 3r/load below. The readout and each point say which regime the relay is in; the single-phasing ceiling (50% only at ≥ 1.5 ×FLA, load/3 below) and the reverse-rotation verdict follow the same rule. |
| 2 | Medium | **32 underpower / reverse power over-injected by 1/cos(MTA).** Current was solved as P/(3V·cos θ) and placed at −θ, so the element's operating quantity P·cosθ + Q·sinθ came out as P/cosθ, not P. At θ = 0 (the default) there is no error; at θ = 45° the "85% — OPERATE" underpower point injects 1.2× pickup and does not operate. | I = P/(3V) at −θ. Desc and readout say what the element compares. Added the electrical rating √3·V·FLA·pf beside the shaft-rating base (see open item 2). |
| 3 | Low | **Dual-CT 87M points silently lost their neutral-end set** outside the 6 × 12.5 A mode: `chans()` dropped the second current set, so the through-current stability point showed a one-sided injection that would operate, against an expected RESTRAIN. | Both sets are always listed; a *Six currents* limit note flags the mode instead. |
| 4 | Low | **Phase rotation hardcoded** outside `P3()`: the 49 half-unbalance set, the 46 sets, the 47 reversal and one-phase-depressed sets, all seven vector presets, and `seq()`. On an ACB relay the 47 reversal point is positive sequence and does not operate; the 46 and 49 unbalanced points carry the wrong sequence; the vector calculator swaps I1 and I2. | `rotS()`/`rotA()`/`P3rev()` helpers everywhere; `seq()` exchanges p and n under ACB. |
| 5 | Low | 51LR plotted every point at the locked-rotor delay (the jam delay was chosen by looking for a letter *s* in the verdict text). Plot only. | Plot time passed explicitly. |
| 6 | Low | Ground and core-balance elements injected on `I L1` with no note saying where it goes; duplicate `inputmode`; no Setup-first on a fresh load. | Fixed as in the 850. |

## Open — needs the 869 manual

1. **IAC Extremely Inverse, constant E.** Same as the 845 and 850: the tool carries
   E = −0.2461 where GE publishes +0.2461. Not changed offline; confirm and correct in
   `CURVES` in every tool that lists it.
2. **32 rated-power base.** Pickup is a percentage of the shaft rating hp × 0.7457
   (1118 kW on the sample). If the relay's per-unit base is the electrical rating
   √3·V·FLA·pf (1154 kW here, +3%) or an entered rated-power setting, the customer scales
   the pickup. A note in the element says so and both figures are on the readout.
3. **Thermal I_M definition.** The tool uses Ieq = √(I1² + K·I2²). GE writes
   I_M·√(1 + K·(I2/I1)²); for the tool's unbalanced construction the average phase
   current equals I1, so they agree unless the relay takes I_M as the highest phase, in
   which case unbalanced timing points come in shorter. The 49 note says so.

## Verified correct (no change)

The standard overload curve t = CM·87.4/(I² − 1) — GE's 2.2116623·CM / (0.02530337(I−1)²
+ 0.05054758(I−1)) form reduces to it within 0.1%, so the default constant is the
relay's own and the fit mode is a refinement, not a correction; hot curve = cold × HCR
(consistent with a steady-state TCU of (1 − HCR) at rated load); IEC replica model;
thermal-capacity accumulation at 100 %/t and exponential decay with the stopped and
running constants; the 48, 66 and 19 sequences and their timer races; 51LR magnitudes
and the start-inhibit point; 37; 50BF; 50P/50G/51G/50SG pickup unit conversions
(×FLA, ×CT, A primary, A secondary through the right CT); IEEE, IEC and IAC curve
equations; 27/59 with the ×VT L-N base; 47 by both methods (V2 = ΔV/3 re-derived);
81 and its voltage supervision; 55 angle sign and current supervision; 87M core-balance
sensitivity through the window CT and the dual-CT slope point (10% above the
characteristic whichever restraint definition the relay uses); the channel-limit checks
and the Setup-tab curve-versus-damage checks.

## Accepted simplifications (documented, not changed)

- Start states are shown at 85% terminal voltage; the current on a reduced-voltage tap
  is taken as LRA × tap fraction.
- The 47 pickup is on the L-N nominal base, matching the 27/59 convention in this tool.
- The core-balance CT secondary is assumed equal to the phase CT secondary.
